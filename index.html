The problem

Smart bottles keep solving the wrong thing.

Most of them add a screen and a subscription to a container that already worked. Then they fail in the three places that actually matter.

  1. 01

    The battery dies when you need it

    A bottle that needs charging every few days is a bottle you stop carrying. Halfway up a trail, a flat battery turns a smart bottle into a heavy dumb one.

  2. 02

    MicroUSB, in the year 2026

    An exposed port on something you fill with water is a design decision that has to be defended. Most of them use a connector the rest of the industry retired years ago.

  3. 03

    They tell you it's cold. They don't keep it warm.

    Reading the temperature is the easy half. Holding it — through a two-hour practice, in the cold — is the half that takes real insulation and real thermal engineering.

What's built in

Engineered around how you actually drink.

Every control is something you can operate one-handed, with cold hands, without looking at a screen.

Product photo · detail
lid and bezel, close crop
  • 01

    Tap-to-glow LED ring

    One tap on the lid and a ring around the cap glows to show the current temperature. It reads at a glance, in daylight or dark, then fades back out on its own.

  • 02

    Rotating bezel

    Turn the bezel to set your warmth target. It's a physical detent you can feel through gloves — the setting lives on the bottle, not in an app you'd have to open first.

  • 03

    Magnetic charging

    The charge contacts are sealed and flush. There is no port to leave open, no rubber flap to tear off, and nothing on the outside for grit and sweat to work into.

  • 04

    Rocker latch spout

    A side-mounted rocker latch with a two-stage safety catch. Flick it and it's open — no twisting a cap off one-handed, and no spring to lose its tension over a season.

  • 05

    UV-C purification, interlocked

    UV-C treats the water inside the bottle. Two independent sensors — one for light, one magnetic — have to agree the cap is sealed before the lamp will fire. If either disagrees, it won't run.

  • 06

    Boost Mode

    Hold the lid for two seconds for a modest active warm-up. It's a few degrees to take the chill off a cold drink, not a kettle — we'd rather say that plainly than oversell it.

Optional companion

PULSE Point

A small wrist accessory that mixes electrolytes on demand, so you can dose mid-run without stopping to tear open a sachet. It pairs with the flask but isn't required by it.

A separate product on its own timeline. The flask comes first.

Why this exists

A note from the founder

I'm 12, based in India, and I play football, badminton, pickleball and cricket. In 2025 I got a bad case of typhoid from contaminated water, and it got me thinking about how something as basic as safe drinking water isn't a given for everyone.

Around the same time I was getting frustrated with my own bottle mid-practice — dead batteries, cold coffee, apps I never open. Both problems led to the same answer.

I designed the full spec myself: features, materials, cost structure. My parent is co-applicant, since I'm under 18.

Pricing

We're transparent about how this is priced.

Hardware companies usually don't show you this. There's no good reason not to, so here is what the flask costs to make and what it will sell for.

Landed cost, retail price and gross margin for PULSE Flask
Landed cost per unit ~$30
Retail price (MSRP) $69 – $84
Gross margin ~59%

Landed cost covers manufacturing plus shipping and duties into market. Final retail will land inside that range once tooling and freight are locked.

The margin isn't profit sitting in a pocket. It pays for warranty replacements, returns, support, amortising the tooling, and funding the next version. We'd rather show the number and explain it than hide it.
