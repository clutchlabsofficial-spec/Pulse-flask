The problem

Smart bottles keep solving the wrong thing.

Most of them add a screen and a subscription to a container that already worked. Then they fail in the three places that actually matter.

  1. 01

    The battery dies when you need it

    A bottle that needs charging every few days is a bottle you stop carrying. Halfway up a trail, a flat battery turns a smart bottle into a heavy dumb one.

  2. 02

    MicroUSB, in the year 2026

    An exposed port on something you fill with water is a design decision that has to be defended. Most of them use a connector the rest of the industry retired years ago.

  3. 03

    They tell you it's cold. They don't keep it warm.

    Reading the temperature is the easy half. Holding it — through a two-hour practice, in the cold — is the half that takes real insulation and real thermal engineering.

How it works

Five things it does, without a screen.

01 / 05

Product photo · showcase
LED ring, lit red

  1. 01

    Tap-to-glow LED ring

    One tap on the lid and the ring around the cap glows to show the current temperature. It reads at a glance, then fades out on its own — an always-on display is why the others are flat by Thursday.

  2. 02

    Rotating bezel

    Turn the bezel to set your warmth target. It clicks through fixed detents you can count by feel through gloves, and the setting lives on the bottle — not in an app you'd have to open first.

  3. 03

    Magnetic charging

    A magnetic puck lands on the twist-off base and self-aligns. The contacts sit flush and sealed — no port to leave open, no rubber flap to tear off, nothing for grit and sweat to work into.

  4. 04

    Rocker latch, two-stage catch

    Flick it and it's open — no twisting a cap off one-handed. The first stage holds the spout shut against knocks in a bag; only a deliberate second movement releases it. No spring to go soft.

  5. 05

    Dual-sensor UV-C

    UV-C treats the water inside the bottle. Two sensors working on different principles — one optical, one magnetic — must both agree the cap is sealed before the lamp fires. Either one disagreeing mid-cycle cuts it immediately.

Why this exists

A note from the founder

I'm 12, based in India, and I play football, badminton, pickleball and cricket. In 2025 I got a bad case of typhoid from contaminated water, and it got me thinking about how something as basic as safe drinking water isn't a given for everyone.

Around the same time I was getting frustrated with my own bottle mid-practice — dead batteries, cold coffee, apps I never open. Both problems led to the same answer.

I designed the full spec myself: features, materials, cost structure. My parent is co-applicant, since I'm under 18.

Pricing

We're transparent about how this is priced.

Hardware companies usually don't show you this. There's no good reason not to, so here is what each size costs to land and what it will sell for.

Landed cost, retail price and gross margin for each PULSE Flask size
Size Landed cost Retail Gross margin
Compact · 500 ml ~$82 $120 32%
Standard · 800 ml ~$117 $165 29%
Pro · 1 L ~$128 $190 33%

Landed cost covers manufacturing plus shipping and duties into market. Final retail will land inside the ranges shown on each size once tooling and freight are locked.

Under a third isn't a large margin for hardware, and that's deliberate — an active heater, a UV-C module and a real battery cost what they cost. What's left pays for warranty replacements, returns, support, amortising the tooling, and funding the next version. We'd rather show the number and explain it than hide it.
